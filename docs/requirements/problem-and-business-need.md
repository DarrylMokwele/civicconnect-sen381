# CivicConnect Problem and Business Need

## Problem Statement

Community service requests are currently received through email, phone calls, WhatsApp, spreadsheets and paper records. Because these channels are separate, requests can be duplicated, missed, assigned incorrectly or lost.

Requesters cannot easily see what is happening with their requests. Staff may struggle to see who is responsible for a request or which requests need attention. Management also lacks one reliable view of open, overdue and completed work.

## Design Problems

1. **Fragmented intake and tracking workflow**
   Multiple intake channels create duplicated and inconsistent records, making it difficult to maintain one reliable request lifecycle from submission to closure. The design must centralise intake and ensure every request has a single traceable record (FR-001, FR-002, FR-004, NFR-003).

2. **Visibility, control and accountability tension**
   The platform must allow requesters, staff and management to see relevant request information while strictly enforcing role boundaries and preserving accountability evidence. The design must balance access control, status governance and reporting without exposing restricted information (FR-003, FR-006, FR-010, FR-013 to FR-016, NFR-002, NFR-005).

3. **Operational responsiveness under constrained resources**
   CivicConnect must remain responsive and usable while handling search, assignment, updates, feedback and oversight workflows in a zero-budget environment. The design must support fast common actions and dependable operational availability under expected usage (FR-005, FR-007, FR-009, NFR-001, NFR-004, NFR-006).

## Business Need

CivicConnect is needed to keep service requests in one system. Requesters need to submit and track requests. Staff need to manage and update them. Management needs reliable information to monitor service delivery.

The system should also make it clear who is responsible for each request and what actions were taken.
